**What the gates stop, and what they do not.** `make docs` (and so CI)
refuses: a step row over 500 characters, with other than four cells, with a
date, with struck-through text, with a quote of the owner, or with one of the
build labels it knows (`Built as`, `not built`, `not met`, `not done`,
`Designed`, `Implemented`, `implemented and tested`, a status word in
backticks); a step table with other columns; a `**Status:**` line or the old
status heading in the plan; an answered or struck-through question in Part D,
an open one in the closed file, a question over 900 characters and a number
in two rows; a part after Part E; and the plan's text outside its rows over
47,600 bytes or more than 2,048 under it. CI refuses a pull request titled for
a step that neither adds nor changes that step's file. They do not stop:

- **A row that is short, clean and wrong.** No check reads prose for truth.
- **A build label in other words** ("implemented, tested", "wired to
  nothing", "ran once on kind"), a date written out ("on the seventh of
  October") and the owner's words without quotation marks.
- **History in the prose between the tables** and in Part A, up to the
  ceiling: the ceiling stops growth, not content.
- **A step's pull request under another title** (`docs: …`), and a title
  edited after the last push, which is read at the next run only.
- **A step file that is touched and says nothing new.** The check sees that
  the file changed, not what was written.

So the `docs-sync` audit before every pull request stays the judgment half,
and Part A says so.
