### S101 — The plan shows no status: the finished steps and those in flight are its last part

**Status:** done · **Started:** 2026-10-08 · **Finished:** 2026-10-08
**Goal:** the plan file reads as the plan: its step tables hold no status, a
step's status lives in the step's own file, and the plan's last part lists the
finished steps and the steps in flight, each linked to its file, so that
finishing a step never edits the plan above that part.

The session's, each with what it rejected:

- **The Status column leaves all seven step tables; "Done when" cells are not
  touched**, also where they carry dates and struck-through text: they are the
  plan's scope, and rewriting them is the owner's. Rejected: cleaning the
  cells of what reads like progress (not provable, and not asked).
- **A step's status is the line under its file's heading, and nowhere else.**
  All 82 files had the line, in one form. Part B gives the check the step
  numbers only. Rejected: a status in both places, held equal by a check (two
  workers that finish would still edit one table of the plan).
- **The 15 status cells that held a note go whole into their step's file**, as
  one paragraph under the status line, "Where it stands:", wrapped at 80
  columns. Rejected: dropping them as copies of what the files say (nobody
  compared them).
- **The last part is generated, not appended by hand.** The owner's "after
  every done steps it should write the bottom section" is kept by a command,
  `make plan-progress`, that writes two tables from the files' status lines,
  and `make docs` fails when they are stale. They are sorted and hold no date
  of the run, so two branches write the same lines from the same files, and a
  merge conflict there is settled by running the command, never by hand.
  Rejected: a list a session appends to (two workers that finish collide in
  one place, which is why the change log became files in S097 and ended in
  S100).
- **"Finished steps", and under it "In flight".** A step whose file says
  `done` is in the first, by the day it finished and then by number, oldest
  first, so a new one lands at the bottom. Every other step that has a file is
  in the second with its word (`doing`, `todo`, `blocked`, `dropped`), by the
  day it started. A step with no file is in neither: it has not started.
- **Part C's hand-kept index is removed.** Every step file is in exactly one
  of the two generated tables with a link, so the index would be a second
  copy. Each of its 82 rows was compared with its file's heading and place
  before it went.
- **The running "Status:" paragraph leaves the plan's top for Part F**
  ("Where the project stands"), word for word. Most steps edited it; at the
  bottom, a step that changes what exists still says so, and the plan's top
  stays as it is. Rejected: keeping it on top (every step would still edit the
  plan's first lines); deleting it for the README's table (loses text).
- **A step is free** when it has no file, or a file that says `todo` (S095
  has one), and every step it depends on is `done` by its own file. This
  replaces "the next `todo` step whose dependencies are `done`" in `CLAUDE.md`,
  `AGENTS.md` and `CONTRIBUTING.md`.
- **The five words of the plan's legend stay**, and the check accepts exactly
  those. No file says `blocked` or `dropped` today.
- **The two plan skills are not changed.** `plan-worker` and `plan-dispatcher`
  name no status of the plan's tables (the dispatcher's "row" is the board's);
  Part A and the check's message tell a worker to run `make plan-progress`.

**For a branch cut before this step** (S021 and S098 were in flight): after
`git merge origin/main`, `make docs` names each thing to do.

1. A conflict in the step's row in Part B: take `main`'s side (four cells, no
   status), and make again any change the branch made to its "Done when"
   cell. A status note the branch wrote in the row goes into the step's file.
2. A conflict in Part C's index: take `main`'s side (the index is gone).
3. A conflict at the plan's top, in the "Status:" paragraph: take `main`'s
   side there, and make the branch's change in Part F, "Where the project
   stands".
4. The step's own status is the line in its file. Then run `make
   plan-progress`; a conflict between Part F's two markers is settled by that
   command, never by hand.
